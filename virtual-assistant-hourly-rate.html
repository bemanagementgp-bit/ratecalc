      Virtual Assistant Hourly Rate Calculator: how it works

      Virtual assistants are often told what the market pays before anyone asks what they need to earn. Platform rates are set by global competition and by assistants who have not counted their own costs; copying them means working more hours for less than a local part-time job.

      This preset assumes a $45,000 take-home goal, light expenses (around $1,800 a year for software, a phone plan and a decent headset), a 20 percent tax rate and six billable hours a day, which suits VAs with two or three steady clients. Lower the hours if you spend a lot of time finding work.

      General admin, specialised support and packages

      Your rate should move with the skill the client is buying:

      • General admin (inbox, calendar, data entry, travel) sits at the lower end of VA rates.
      • Specialised support such as bookkeeping, podcast production, social media management, CRM administration or customer support in a second language commands 30 to 100 percent more, because it replaces a specialist, not an assistant.
      • Monthly packages (for example 20 hours a month, paid in advance) are how experienced VAs stabilise income. Price them at your hourly rate times the hours, with a small discount for prepayment and a clear policy for unused and extra hours.

      Avoiding the two classic VA pricing traps

      The first trap is unpaid availability: being on call across a client's whole working day while billing only the minutes you were busy. Either sell blocks of availability or set response-time expectations in writing. The second trap is scope creep: a calendar client who starts sending research tasks and then bookkeeping. Keep a short services list with rates per category and re-quote when the work changes.

      Review your rate every six months. Each time you add a tool, a skill or a testimonial, you are a different service than the one you priced originally. Use the share link to send a client a transparent breakdown when you announce an increase.

      Frequently asked questions

      What is a good hourly rate for a virtual assistant?

      General VAs in the US, UK, Canada and Australia typically charge $25 to $45 an hour; specialised VAs charge $45 to $80 or more. Rates in other markets differ, so calculate from your own income goal and costs.

      Should I charge less while I build experience?

      A slightly lower starting rate for your first two or three clients is reasonable, but set it from the calculator's floor, not from the cheapest platform listing, and schedule the increase in advance.

      How do I price a retainer package?

      Multiply the monthly hours by your hourly rate, apply a small prepayment discount if you like, and state what happens with unused hours. The calculator's quote box gives you the base figure.

      Is my data private?

      Yes. RateCalc runs entirely in your browser; your numbers are stored only on your device.
