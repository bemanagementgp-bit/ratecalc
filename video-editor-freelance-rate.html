      Freelance Video Editor Rate Calculator: how it works

      Video editing has unusually high overheads for a desk job: a fast workstation, fast storage, plug-ins, music and stock licences, and a software stack that bills monthly whether you are cutting or not. Rates that ignore those costs feel generous per hour and leave nothing to replace a drive when it dies mid-project.

      The preset uses a $70,000 take-home target, about $8,000 a year in equipment depreciation and subscriptions, a 25 percent tax rate and five billable hours a day. Render and export time counts as billable only if you are actively working on the project while it runs.

      Pricing by hour, by finished minute or by project

      Editors use three units, and clients will push for whichever looks cheapest. Anchor all three to the hourly figure from the calculator:

      • Hourly for open-ended work: re-cuts, feedback-heavy corporate projects, assisting on someone else's edit.
      • Per finished minute for predictable formats. Time a few past jobs: a talking-head YouTube video often needs 1 to 2 hours per finished minute; motion-heavy social ads can need 4 to 8. Multiply by your rate.
      • Per project for packages (a wedding film, a course of ten lessons) with a stated number of revision rounds and extra rounds billed hourly.

      The project quote box applies a rush multiplier, useful when a client needs the cut by tomorrow morning.

      Costs and non-billable time specific to editors

      Put a realistic depreciation figure in expenses: a $4,000 workstation and $1,500 of drives replaced every three years is already $1,800 a year before software, plug-ins and licences. Then think about non-billable time: ingesting and backing up footage, downloading client files, syncing audio and waiting for feedback are rarely itemised but always happen. Either bill them as a flat media-management fee or keep your billable hours per day honest so the rate absorbs them.

      Finally, protect the rate with terms: footage delivered organised and named, a defined number of revision rounds, and a 50 percent deposit before the first cut.

      Frequently asked questions

      What do freelance video editors charge per hour?

      Commonly $40 to $100 an hour, with experienced editors on commercial, broadcast or motion-graphics work charging $100 to $175. Market and niche matter more than years of experience.

      How much should I charge per finished minute of video?

      Multiply the hours a minute of that format takes you by your hourly rate. For a simple talking-head edit at $95 an hour and 1.5 hours per minute, that is about $140 per finished minute.

      Should I charge a rush fee?

      Yes, typically 25 to 50 percent extra for turnarounds under 48 hours. Set the rush multiplier in the quote box to see the effect on a specific job.

      Can I save different rates for different kinds of work?

      With RateCalc Pro you can save named profiles (for example YouTube edits, commercial work) and compare them side by side.
