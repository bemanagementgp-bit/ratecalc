      Freelance Web Developer Hourly Rate Calculator: how it works

      Most developers set their first freelance rate by dividing an old salary by 2,080 hours. That number ignores the hours you spend on proposals, calls, invoicing and learning the next framework, and it ignores that nobody pays your health insurance or your laptop anymore. The result is a rate that looks fine in January and feels like a pay cut by June.

      RateCalc starts from what you want to take home and works backwards. We preloaded it with numbers typical for a mid-level freelance web developer in the US (an $85,000 target, about $5,000 a year in tooling, five billable hours a day). Change anything and the hourly, daily and project figures update instantly, in your browser.

      What drives a developer's hourly rate

      Three inputs move the number more than anything else:

      • Billable hours per day. Developers who track time honestly land between four and six billable hours on a good day. Code review, Slack, estimates and context-switching eat the rest. Enter five, not eight.
      • Expenses. Hosting for demos, a good machine every three years, paid APIs, courses, an accountant, coworking. $4,000 to $8,000 a year is normal; add health insurance if you pay it yourself.
      • Taxes and buffer. Self-employment tax plus income tax commonly takes 25 to 35 percent in the US. The buffer covers the quiet month between contracts and the client who pays 60 days late.

      With the preset values the calculator lands around $110 an hour, because five billable hours and a 25 percent tax rate leave little room. Senior or specialised work (performance, security, legacy migrations) routinely commands $120 to $200.

      Hourly, day rate or fixed price?

      Hourly is the easiest to start with and the easiest for clients to compare, which is exactly why it caps your income. Once you can estimate a type of project reliably (a marketing site, a Shopify theme, an API integration), switch to fixed prices built from your hourly rate: multiply estimated hours by your rate, then add 20 to 50 percent for the risk you are absorbing. The project quote box below the main result does this for you.

      Retainers suit maintenance and ongoing feature work: a set number of hours per month, paid up front, at a small discount. They smooth your cash flow and reduce the non-billable sales time that forces your rate up in the first place.

      Frequently asked questions

      What is a typical freelance web developer hourly rate?

      In the US and Western Europe, generalist freelance developers usually charge between $60 and $120 an hour; specialists charge $120 to $200 or more. Rates vary a lot by market and experience, so calculate yours from your own costs rather than copying an average.

      Should I charge the same rate for every client?

      Your floor should be the same, because your costs are. Above the floor you can price by value: a checkout fix for a store doing $2M a year is worth more than the same hours for a hobby site.

      How do I handle a client who wants a discount?

      Reduce scope, not the rate. Removing a feature or a revision round keeps your hourly value intact; lowering the rate resets the anchor for every future project with that client.

      Does the calculator store my numbers?

      Only in your browser's local storage so you find them again next visit. Nothing is uploaded, and the share link simply encodes your inputs in the URL.
